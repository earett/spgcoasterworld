      Case file

        Teacher Guide

        At a glance

        SPG Coasterworld is a free, browser-based roller coaster lab where students design rides and use real physics to make them work. It runs at earett.github.io/spgcoasterworld on any Chromebook or laptop, with no logins or accounts. SPG Coasterworld collects no student data: nothing students type or build ever leaves their device.

        • Four modes: Build Mode, Story Mode, Launch Lab and Ride Inspector, each a tab at the top of the page.
        • Progress saves in each student’s browser. Switching devices or clearing browser data starts them over, and nothing reports back to the teacher.
        • Reset all (top right) erases everything on that device. Have students click it at the end of class on shared devices.
        • Light and dark mode: the button in the header switches between them, and the choice is remembered.

        Data & privacy

        SPG Coasterworld was built so that you can use it with students without collecting anything about them.

        • No accounts. Students never sign up, log in, or enter an email address.
        • No tracking. There are no analytics, ads, tracking cookies or third-party trackers.
        • Progress stays on the device. Designs, missions, Story Mode money and settings are saved in the browser’s own storage on that Chromebook. They are not sent to you, to Ms. Arett, or to any server, and no one else can see them.
        • Coaster names stay on the student’s screen. There is no leaderboard or sharing. Still, remind students to use made-up coaster names rather than their own full names.
        • What any website sees. The page is hosted on GitHub Pages and its fonts come from Google Fonts. Like any website, those services receive basic technical information when the page loads, such as the device’s IP address and browser type. They do not receive anything students type or build.
        • Deleting data. Reset all erases everything on that device. Clearing the browser’s site data does the same.

        Follow your district’s usual process for approving new websites. This section describes how the site works; it isn’t legal advice.

        Suggested 50-minute lessons

        Each plan stands alone; together they make a four-day mini unit.

        Day 1: Build and break (Build Mode)

        1. Hook, 5 min: project a crash. Ask “What went wrong?”
        2. Free build, 15 min: students name their coaster, pick a mascot, and get a ride to pass inspection.
        3. Challenge, 15 min: load the “Too tight” and “Hill too tall” presets and fix them.
        4. Share out, 10 min: screenshot the Trailblazer Times front page. Discuss what made rides intense or stall.
        5. Exit ticket, 5 min: “Why can’t a hill after the lift be taller than the lift?”

        Day 2: F = ma (Launch Lab)

        1. Mini-lesson, 10 min: F = m × a, solving for each variable.
        2. Classic missions 1–4, 20 min: everyone has the same numbers, so pairs can compare.
        3. Classic missions 5–8, 15 min: early finishers switch to Challenge.
        4. Debrief, 5 min: “Same force, double the mass. What happened?” (mission 7)

        Day 3: Newton’s laws (Ride Inspector)

        1. Mini-lesson, 10 min: same object (balanced forces) vs. two objects pushing on each other (action/reaction).
        2. Inspection, 25 min: students inspect their Day 1 coaster and answer the writing prompts on paper.
        3. Share, 10 min: compare answers for the brakes stop, where all three laws act at once.
        4. Turn in, 5 min: certificate screenshot plus written responses.

        Days 4+: Story Mode project

        Students work through clients at their own pace. Side jobs give F = ma and net force practice. Good for choice time or a multi-day design challenge.

        MisconceptionWhere students run into itWhat they see

        “If it’s moving, the forces must be unbalanced.”Ride Inspector stop 2 (lift hill)Steady speed means balanced forces, even while moving
        “Equal and opposite forces are always the 3rd law.”Ride Inspector stops 1 and 2Targeted feedback: balanced forces act on the same object; action/reaction pairs act on two objects
        “Inertia is a force that pushes you.”Ride Inspector stops 3 and 6Part A choices separate “inertia keeps motion the same” from forces that change it

        Physics simplifications to know

        The physics is real, but each mode simplifies differently, and sharp students may notice.

        Build Mode and Story ModeLaunch Lab
        Gravity9.81 m/s²Rounded to 10 m/s² so the math stays friendly
        FrictionRolling friction and air resistance, adjustableNone on the launch track (magnetic motors)
        Speed unitsmph by default; m/s with Show real numbersm/s
        BrakesFixed slowing rate; trains over 43 mph fly offFixed brake force, so heavier trains need more room to stop
        • Rider feel thresholds: Comfy up to 3.5 g, Intense from 3.5 to 5 g (or below −1 g), Ouch above 5 g (or below −1.5 g). Floating, or a “stomach drop,” is under 0.2 g.
        • Things that don’t affect the physics: mascot themes, money, reputation and chance cards are game layers only.
        • Mass: in Build and Story Mode, mass only matters slightly through air resistance. In Launch Lab, mass matters directly through a = F ÷ m. That contrast is worth discussing.

        Troubleshooting and safety

        ProblemFix
        A student lost their progressProgress lives in that browser only. Same Chromebook and same login are needed to keep it.
        The page shows an old version after an updateHard refresh: Ctrl + Shift + R.
        Ride Inspector says “Not ready for inspection”The Build Mode coaster stalls or flies off. Fix it in Build Mode, or inspect the starter coaster.
        Story Mode clients are lockedThe company is in debt. Students earn their way out with side jobs.
        Animations stutterClose other tabs. Older Chromebooks may run slower at 4× playback.

        Coaster name filter: names are checked for swears and slurs in English and Spanish, including number swaps, stretched letters and spaced-out letters. Blocked names never save. No filter catches everything, so report anything that slips through so it can be added. Names only appear on the student’s own screen; there is no shared leaderboard.

        Easter egg: a coaster name with 67 in it makes 67s rain down the screen. It’s harmless, and you’ve been warned.
